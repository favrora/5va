Make the Most of a Handyman Visit: A Simple Checklist

A few photos, a clear priority list, and the right product details help turn a collection of small home jobs into a well-planned visit.

Write down the jobs, then choose priorities

The loose handle you keep noticing is easy to remember. The sticking cabinet and unassembled shelf are easier to forget until after the visit. Start with one list and give each task a simple description. Separate what needs attention first from what would be nice to finish if the agreed schedule allows.

Share the list before the appointment so there is time to discuss the surfaces, products, and materials involved. Photos of the surrounding area are useful as well as close-ups of a problem. A little preparation helps organize the work and avoid buying supplies that do not fit.

Send the information that saves guesswork

Take one close photo of the issue and one wider photo showing the surrounding area. For furniture, include a product link, dimensions, and assembly instructions. For mounting, show the intended location and describe the wall if you know its construction. Avoid drilling test holes just to find out.

Keep supplied brackets, fasteners, and instructions together. Do not assume that hardware included in a box is suitable for every wall. Manufacturer requirements, item weight, fixing points, and access all affect whether a mounting job is appropriate.

For a longer list, start with the small home repair planner. If furniture is part of the visit, review the furniture assembly guide for product, wall and access details.

Prepare the space and permissions

Clear a practical route to the work area and move fragile belongings if you can do so safely. Leave heavy items in place unless a different arrangement has been agreed. Mention stairs, parking restrictions, building access, pets, and any limited work hours when discussing the appointment.

If you rent or live in a managed building, arrange the needed permission before drilling or changing fixtures. Share any building work hours or access arrangements in advance. This keeps the visit focused on the jobs you want finished instead of paperwork or a locked entrance.

Agree on the finish, not just the start

Before work begins, review the planned tasks, supplied materials, expected cost, and what happens if conditions differ from the initial description. For furniture, discuss any manufacturer-required anti-tip anchoring. CPSC recommends securing suitable furniture and TVs to help prevent tip-over injuries; assembly and safe installation should be considered together.

At the end, review the completed tasks and any remaining items. Keep product instructions and ask about normal use or follow-up needs. To plan a visit with 5VA, send your task list, photos, and Philadelphia-region ZIP code. We will talk through the work and appointment availability with you.
