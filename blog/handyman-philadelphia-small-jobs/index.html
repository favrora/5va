Start with what would make the home easier to use

A sticking bedroom door, a loose cabinet handle, and a shelf still in its box do not each need a separate planning process. They need a clear list. When looking for a handyman in Philadelphia, begin with the everyday frustrations you want solved rather than trying to name the exact repair. A description such as the bathroom cabinet door drops when opened is more useful than a guess about the hinge model.

Walk through the rooms and record the tasks as you notice them. Give each an outcome: the door closes without rubbing, the wall looks finished after the old bracket is removed, or the desk is ready to use. Add one close photo and one wider photo. This shows the work area, nearby furniture, and details that a close-up alone can hide.

Separate priorities from optional extras

Mark the two or three jobs that matter most. Then add the tasks you would like to include if the agreed visit allows. This prevents a long list from obscuring the reason you called. A loose cabinet handle and a new curtain rod might belong in the same plan, while a wall repair that needs a particular finish may need its own material discussion.

Keep developing problems separate from cosmetic work. A damp patch, a door that has suddenly changed shape, or damage that keeps returning deserves an explanation before it is covered or adjusted. Active flooding, smoke, or an electrical hazard is not a routine maintenance-list item. Get help appropriate to the immediate problem instead of waiting for a general handyman appointment.

If your list includes a damaged wall, use our drywall repair planning guide, then review what to prepare before a handyman visit to make the first conversation more useful.

Make the request specific enough to plan

A useful request includes the property ZIP, task list, photos, preferred timing, and any materials already purchased. For mounting, share the item dimensions and product link. For an adjustment, explain when the issue began and whether it happens every day. If you have a paint label, spare part, or assembly manual, mention it before ordering something new.

For example, a planning list could include one interior door that rubs at the upper corner, two loose cabinet pulls, a small wall repair where a shelf was removed, and assembly of a specific flat-pack desk. That is an illustrative list, not a record of a 5VA customer job. The value is in the detail: the person planning the work can ask useful questions before the visit.

Understand the difference between calling and booking work

5VA does not charge for the phone conversation. That does not make an on-site visit, diagnosis, materials, or completed work free. Ask what is included in the proposed visit, whether any assessment fee applies, and how the work will be priced. You should understand the next step before reserving time or buying supplies.

Photos can help plan a job, but they cannot reveal everything behind a wall or inside worn hardware. Discuss how a change will be handled if the condition differs from the description. It is also useful to ask whether the proposed plan covers the entire list or only the priority tasks. Adding a new job during the visit should involve another conversation about time, materials, and cost.

Account for the way your Philadelphia property is accessed

Mention a narrow staircase, upper-floor room, shared entrance, limited parking, or building work hours. If the job is in a rowhome, show the route to the work area when a large assembled item or long piece of material is involved. If it is in an apartment, arrange building access and any permission needed for mounting or changing fixtures.

You do not need to reorganize the whole home. Clear a practical workspace, move small fragile items, and keep pets away from the work area. Leave heavy appliances and furniture in place unless another arrangement has been agreed. For a rental property, name the person who can approve the work and make sure the tenant knows how and when access is arranged.

Finish with a list you can check together

Before the work starts, review the planned tasks, materials, expected cost, and desired finish. At the end, walk through the results and record anything left for a future visit. Save product instructions and paint information with the task list. The City of Philadelphia Rowhouse Manual is a useful broader reference for organizing ongoing care of a rowhome.
