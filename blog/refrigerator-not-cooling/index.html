Refrigerator Not Cooling? Start Here

A calm, practical checklist for a warm refrigerator: protect your food, check the basics, and know what to tell the repair technician.

Protect your food, record what the refrigerator is doing, and check only the visible controls, door closure, and air vents before arranging an assessment.

Protect what is inside first

Before troubleshooting, think about the food. Keep the doors closed as much as possible and move vulnerable items to a reliably cold alternative when you can. A light inside the refrigerator does not tell you whether food is being held at a safe temperature. Use a suitable appliance thermometer and the official FoodSafety.gov guidance linked below when deciding what to keep.

Make a note of when you first noticed the problem. If the temperature history is uncertain, do not rely on taste or smell to decide whether perishable food is safe. Getting a repair scheduled and making a food-safety decision are separate tasks.

Check the simple things you can see

Look at the temperature controls and compare them with the owner’s manual. A control may have been changed, or the appliance may have entered a special mode. Read any message before pressing buttons so you can describe it accurately later.

Check that packages are not preventing the doors from closing and that food is not crowded directly against visible interior air vents. These observations do not require removing panels. Avoid pulling a heavy refrigerator out or disturbing water connections just to investigate.

If an assessment identifies a repair, our repair-or-replace guide explains the wider costs to compare. For a second appliance problem, see what to note when a washer will not drain before discussing the visit.

Notice the pattern

Is the freezer still cold while the fresh-food compartment is warm? Are both sections affected? Did the issue start after a power interruption, a move, or loading a large grocery order? The difference matters more than a guess about a compressor or thermostat.

Photograph any displayed error code and record new noises or visible frost. Consult the model-specific manual for normal operation and recovery times. Do not chip away ice, heat frozen components, open electrical covers, or try refrigerant work.

Make the service call useful

Have the brand, model, ZIP code, and a short description ready. Mention whether food has already been moved and whether the appliance is built in or difficult to access. A concise history helps plan an assessment; it does not replace one.

For Philadelphia or Feasterville-area service, contact 5VA Appliance Repair to confirm coverage and availability. Once the cause has been assessed, discuss the proposed work and expected cost before deciding how to proceed. Keep following food-safety guidance while waiting for service.
